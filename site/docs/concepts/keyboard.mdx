## The active cell

One cell at a time is **active**: it has `data-active` and is the grid's tab stop. The keys move
it, the grid scrolls it into view and focuses it.

| key | moves to |
| --- | --- |
| Arrows | the next cell in that direction (header rows are -1 and above), through the [summary rows](/docs/concepts/summary-rows) as they show |
| Home, End | the row's first or last cell |
| Ctrl+Home, Ctrl+End | the grid's first or last cell (a bottom summary row's, with some) |
| PageUp, PageDown | a page of rows up or down, in the body |
| Tab | leaves the grid |
| Shift+Space | selects the active row, or clears it ([row selection](/docs/concepts/row-selection) on) |
| Shift+↑, Shift+↓ | the next row, selecting from the anchor (many rows selectable) |
| Ctrl+A, ⌘+A | selects every row (many rows selectable) |
| Shift+arrows, Shift+Home, Shift+End, Shift+PageUp, Shift+PageDown | move the selected range's focus, the active cell staying its anchor ([cell selection](/docs/concepts/cell-selection) on; then Shift+↑, Shift+↓ and Ctrl+A are the cells', Shift+Space stays the row's) |
| Ctrl+Shift+Home, Ctrl+Shift+End | take the range's focus to the first or the last cell (cell selection on) |
| Ctrl+A, ⌘+A | selects every body cell (cell selection on) |
| Escape | clears the selected range; a plain move clears it too |
| Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V | copy the range (or the active cell) as tab-separated text, paste into it (cell selection on; ⌘ on a Mac) |
| Enter, F2, a printable key | on an [editable](/docs/concepts/editing) cell, edit it (the printable key starts the editor; a character typed with AltGr or Option too) |
| Enter, Shift+Enter, Tab, Shift+Tab | in an edit, commit it and move down, up, to the next or the previous column |
| Escape | in an edit, cancel it, focus back on the cell; during a [fill](/docs/concepts/fill)'s drag, cancel it |
| Ctrl+Shift+←, Ctrl+Shift+→ | moves a reorderable header cell's column or group, the active cell following it (⌘ on a Mac) |
| Ctrl+Shift+↑, Ctrl+Shift+↓ | moves the active cell's row up or down, the active cell following it ([row reordering](/docs/concepts/row-reordering) on; ⌘ on a Mac) |
| Enter, Space | on a group row, expands or collapses it ([row grouping](/docs/concepts/row-grouping)); Space on a tree's parent too ([tree data](/docs/concepts/tree-data)) |
| →, ← | on a row's tree cell in a `treegrid` (the one holding its toggle), expands a collapsed group, collapses an expanded one, or goes to the row it is under |

Moves stop at the edges. In a [right-to-left](/docs/concepts/right-to-left) grid the arrows
mirror: ← moves to the next column and → to the previous one, as they point; Home and End stay the
row's first and last cell, Ctrl+Shift+← moves a header cell after its next sibling, and on a resize
handle ← moves it to the left, which widens its column. A click or a Tab into a cell makes it active. On a sortable column's
header cell, Enter and Space toggle its [sort](/docs/concepts/sorting) (Ctrl adds the column).
On a [reorderable](/docs/concepts/column-reordering) header cell, Ctrl+Shift+← and Ctrl+Shift+→
move its column (or group) before the previous sibling or after the next one, focus staying on
it; elsewhere they move the active cell as the arrows do. While the rows
[move](/docs/concepts/row-reordering), Ctrl+Shift+↑ and Ctrl+Shift+↓ on a body cell move its row
by one, once per press, focus following it once you have moved it (with a `rowKey`; nothing moves
while the grid is sorted); the same right to left.

<Example id="keyboard-navigation" />

With [column groups](/docs/concepts/column-groups), the header has several rows: Up from a column
reaches the group above it, Down from a group its first column in view, and Left and Right move
across the cells of a header row. A cell [spanning columns](/docs/concepts/column-spanning) is one
cell to the keys: an arrow into a column it covers lands on it, and the arrows leaving it go to
the column past its span. The [summary rows](/docs/concepts/summary-rows) are rows to the keys:
Up from the first body row reaches the top ones, Down from the last the bottom ones.

With [row groups](/docs/concepts/row-grouping) the grid is a `treegrid` and the keys are the APG
treegrid's: Enter and Space on a group row toggle it (F2 hands its controls the keys), Space a
tree's parent, and on a row's tree cell (the one holding its toggle; for a row with none, the
column the toggles are in, else the first) → expands a collapsed group (then moves, as anywhere
else), ← collapses an expanded one, else goes to the row it is under. Shift+Space on a group row
selects its rows.

## One tab stop

While no cell is active, the grid is the tab stop; focusing it activates the first cell in view.
Then the active cell is (`tabIndex` 0, the others -1), so Tab moves past the grid in one step.
The controls inside cells (buttons, links, fields, a select, a box to check) are no stops of their
own: the grid keeps them at `tabIndex` -1, the ones your cells render later included, so a grid
full of controls is still one stop. A grid nested in another one's cell is reachable through
that cell: its stop is in the tab order only while the outer active cell holds it
([Nested grids](/docs/guides/nested-grids)).

## Controls in cells

A cell's controls take the keys in **interaction**, the grid's other mode beside navigation:

| key | in navigation | in interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter, F2 | the active cell's controls take the keys, the first takes focus | the control's |
| Tab, Shift+Tab | leave the grid | the cell's next or previous control, round and round |
| Escape | — | back to navigation, focus on the cell |
| Arrows, Home, End… | move the active cell | the control's (a field's caret, a select's options) |

A click on a control does what Enter does: its cell becomes active and its controls take the keys.
Focus leaving the cell (a click elsewhere, a menu portalled out of it) goes back to navigation. On
a sortable column's header cell, Enter sorts, and F2 reaches its controls. A cell in interaction
carries `data-interacting`, and `useCell` and `useHeaderCell` report `interacting`.

<Example id="interactive-cells" />

A control you want to stay a tab stop of its own carries `data-grid-tab-stop`: the grid leaves its
`tabIndex` alone. From code, `engine.run("interact-cell", { rowIndex, columnIndex })` and
`engine.run("leave-cell", {})` do what Enter and Escape do, and `engine.get("interaction")` tells
which cell is in interaction.

### Resize handles

A [column's resize handle](/docs/concepts/column-resizing) is a control of its header cell: F2
(or Enter, where the header does not sort) hands it the keys, or Tab reaches it among the cell's
other controls. Then, on the focused handle:

| key | does |
| --- | --- |
| ←, → | moves the handle 10 px its way: the column wider where it grows that way (to the right, or to the left right to left; toward the start for a column pinned at the end) |
| Shift+←, Shift+→ | the same by 50 px |
| Home, End | the column's minimum, its maximum (its `aria-valuemax`: the view's width, without a `maxWidth`) |
| Enter | fits the column to its content ([Automatic widths](/docs/concepts/auto-widths)) |
| Escape | back to navigation; the width stays |

The other page keys and Space do nothing on a handle: the grid's container never pages under it.
Escape also cancels a drag, putting back the width it started from.

## Whose key it is

The grid takes keys pressed on its cells, on the grid itself and on its scroll container; in
interaction, only Escape and Tab from the cell's controls. A key pressed on your own content beside
the cells, such as a button in [`DataGrid.Empty`](/docs/guides/empty-state) or anything in a
row's [detail](/docs/guides/master-detail), or in a menu portalled out of a cell, stays with
that content. The arrows move between rows' cells and never land in a detail.

## Replacing a key

A cell's `onKeyDown` runs before the grid's: `preventDefault` cancels the grid's handling, and
`useDataGrid().model.run("active-position.set", …)` moves the cell where you want. The
[`custom-navigation`](/examples/custom-navigation) example makes Tab move across rows this way.

A middleware can refuse or rewrite any move:

```ts title="no-header.ts"
import { veto } from "@fragiola/data-grid-react";

// ArrowUp from the first row stays there: the header never becomes active
model.use((ctx, next) =>
    ctx.command === "active-position.move" &&
    ctx.payload.direction === "up" &&
    ctx.state.activePosition?.rowIndex === 0
        ? veto("the header is not navigable")
        : next(),
);
```

## ARIA

The grid is `role="grid"` with `aria-rowcount` and `aria-colcount` counting every row and column,
rendered or not; rows and cells carry `aria-rowindex` and `aria-colindex`. Name the grid with
`aria-label`: the package sets no names. See [State attributes](/docs/concepts/state-attributes).
