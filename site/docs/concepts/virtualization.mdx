A grid of a million rows by a thousand columns renders a few hundred cells: the ones in view and
a few around them. The rest exists only as size.

<Example id="large-dataset" />

## Windows

On each axis, the engine computes a **window**:

- `visible`: the items in view (an item partly in view counts);
- `rendered`: the visible items plus an **overscan** on each side (4 rows and 2 columns by
  default, `overscan={{ rows, columns }}` to change it).

Ranges are `{ start, end }`, `end` excluded. Read them with `onRowWindowChange` and
`onColumnWindowChange` (they fire when a range changes), with `useRowWindow()` and
`useColumnWindow()` inside the grid, or with `engine.get("row-window")`.

## Scrolling inside the overscan renders nothing

The rendered range is kept while the view stays inside it: scrolling a row or two renders
nothing in React. Once the view leaves it, the rendered range is recomputed around the view.

## The active cell stays rendered

The active row and column are rendered even out of the window, so focus never falls to the
page while you scroll away and back.

## Sizes

Row offsets come from `rowHeight`: one number costs nothing at any count, and a function is
summed once into an array of offsets and searched in O(log n). See the
[`variable-row-height`](/examples/variable-row-height) and
[`many-columns`](/examples/many-columns) examples.
Rows measured from their content (`rowHeight="auto"`) keep the heights measured as a sorted list
over the estimate, also searched in O(log n): see [Measured heights](/docs/concepts/measured-heights).
