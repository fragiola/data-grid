---
title: The primitive contract
description: The rules every part follows, render instead of asChild, merged refs and handlers, class and style functions, and structural style only.
---

Every part of `DataGrid` (`Root`, `Grid`, `Header`, `HeaderRows`, `HeaderRow`, `HeaderCell`,
`Body`, `Row`, `Cell`, `Empty`) follows the same rules.

## `render`, never `asChild`

`render` replaces the element a part renders. Give it an element, and the part's props are merged
into it; or a function, which receives the props and the part's state.

```tsx title="render.tsx"
<DataGrid.Grid render={<table />} aria-label="People" />

<DataGrid.Cell
    cell={cell}
    render={(props, state) => <td {...props} title={state.active ? "active" : undefined} />}
/>
```

## Props, refs and handlers

- Any prop a part does not use is forwarded to its element (`id`, `aria-*`, `data-*`, handlers).
- `ref` is a plain prop, merged with the part's own.
- A handler you pass runs after the part's own, with four exceptions: `Root` runs the grid's keys
  **after** your `onKeyDown`, its header clicks (sorting) **after** your `onClick`, a press
  on a column resizer, a reorderable header cell, a row's drag handle, a fill handle or a body cell with
  [cell selection](/docs/concepts/cell-selection) on (a drag) **after** your `onPointerDown`, and
  a copy or a paste on a cell **after** your `onCopy` or `onPaste`, on `Root` or on its `render`
  element; a cell's handlers run before them, so `preventDefault` cancels a key, a sort, a drag
  or the grid's use of the clipboard. See
  [Keyboard and accessibility](/docs/concepts/keyboard), [Sorting](/docs/concepts/sorting),
  [Column resizing](/docs/concepts/column-resizing),
  [Column reordering](/docs/concepts/column-reordering),
  [Row reordering](/docs/concepts/row-reordering) and
  [Cell selection](/docs/concepts/cell-selection).
- `Body` and `HeaderRow` drop a `transform` from your style: the grid moves them with it. A
  pinned column's cells drop a `transform` and the insets (`top`, `left`, `right`, `bottom`,
  `inset`): they are sticky, the grid writes their `left`. A row's detail drops them too, for the
  same reason.

## `className` and `style`

Both take a value, or a function of the part's state:

```tsx title="row.tsx"
<DataGrid.Row row={row} className={(state) => (state.rowIndex % 2 ? "bg-zinc-50" : undefined)} />
```

## Structural style only

The only inline style a part sets is what places it: `position` (`sticky` on the header, the
empty state, [pinned](/docs/concepts/pinned-columns) cells, a row's
[detail](/docs/guides/master-detail) and a group's
[label](/docs/concepts/collapsible-groups#sticky-labels)), `top`, `left`, `width`, `height`, the layers'
`transform`, `display` (`flex` on rows with pinned columns, `grid` on
[measured](/docs/concepts/measured-heights) rows), the root's `overflow`,
`box-sizing`, a `z-index` that stacks the grid's layers (the header above the summary rows, both
above the body; with
[column groups](/docs/concepts/column-groups), an upper header row above the next), and on a detail `margin-top` (below its row's cells) and,
beside pinned cells, `margin-left` and `flex-shrink`. In a measured row, its cells and detail take
a `grid-area` and the cells their place as an inline start margin, with no height; a measured
detail beside pinned cells takes `align-self`. Your style is
merged under these keys, which always win. Nothing cosmetic is ever set: see the
[`unstyled`](/examples/unstyled) example.

## No text and no names

Parts render their children, or the column's renderer (an
[edited](/docs/concepts/editing) cell its column's `renderEditCell`, your editor). They set no `aria-label` of their own:
name the grid yourself (`<DataGrid.Grid aria-label="People">`). A
[column's resize handle](/docs/concepts/column-resizing) is an element of yours too:
`useColumnResizer` gives it its props (ARIA, `data-*`, the marker the grid finds it by), never a
name, a place or a look, and so does `useRowDragHandle` for a
[row's drag handle](/docs/concepts/row-reordering) (hidden from screen readers: the keys move a
row from its cells), and `useGroupToggle` for a [group row's toggle](/docs/concepts/row-grouping)
(its `aria-expanded`, never a name), and `useFillHandle` for a
[fill handle](/docs/concepts/fill) (its mark, at a range's corner only).

## The recursion is yours

`Rows`, `Cells`, `HeaderRows` and `HeaderCells` take a function that renders each item, so every
row and cell is written by you. Without it, they render the default `Row`, `Cell`, `HeaderRow`
and `HeaderCell`.

## In Vue

`@fragiola/data-grid-vue` follows the same contract with Vue's own means; the parts render the
same elements, attributes and structural style ([Vue primitives](/docs/api/vue)).

- **`as` and the `render` slot, never `asChild`.** `as` takes a tag or a component. The `render`
  scoped slot receives `{ props, state, children }`: bind `props` on your own element, and
  render the part's content with `<component :is="children" />`.

```vue title="cell.vue"
<DataGrid.Grid as="table" aria-label="People" />

<DataGrid.Cell :cell="cell">
    <template #render="{ props, state, children }">
        <td v-bind="props" :title="state.active ? 'active' : undefined">
            <component :is="children" />
        </td>
    </template>
</DataGrid.Cell>
```

- **`class` and `style` are fallthrough attributes**, merged under the structural style, as in
  React (the layers and the pinned cells drop the same keys). There is no function form: style
  the state with the `data-*` attributes, or with the `render` slot's `state`.
- **Listeners compose in the same order**: the part's own first, then yours (`@keydown`,
  `@click`, `@pointerdown`, `@copy`, `@paste`), then the grid's on `Root`, so `preventDefault`
  in yours cancels a key, a sort, a drag or the grid's use of the clipboard.
- **The recursion is slots**: the default scoped slot is the children function
  (`<DataGrid.Rows v-slot="{ row }">`, `<DataGrid.Cells v-slot="{ cell }">`), and a column's
  renderers return vnodes (`Column<TRow, VNodeChild>`).
- **Composables return computed refs**: a part composable (`useRow`, `useCell`,
  `useColumnResizer`, …) takes a value or a getter and returns `{ state, props }`, bound with
  `v-bind="props.value"`.
- **A template ref on a part is its component instance**: its element is `$el`; a handle on the
  grid is a `gridRef` (`useDataGridRef()`).

## In Angular

`@fragiola/data-grid-angular` follows the same contract with Angular's own means; the parts put
the same attributes and structural style on your elements ([Angular directives](/docs/api/angular)).

- **Your element, never `asChild`.** A part is an attribute directive on the element you write
  (`<table dataGridGrid>`, `<td [dataGridCell]="cell">`), the Angular form of `render`. A part
  that renders only sometimes is a structural directive on its element (`*dataGridHeader`,
  `*dataGridEmpty`, `*dataGridRowDetail`, `*dataGridSummary="'top'"`).

```html title="people-grid.ts"
<table dataGridGrid aria-label="People">
    <tbody dataGridBody>
        <ng-template [dataGridRows]="columns" let-row>
            <tr [dataGridRow]="row">
                <ng-template [dataGridCells]="columns" let-cell>
                    <td [dataGridCell]="cell" #own="dataGridCell" [title]="own.state()?.active ? 'active' : ''">
                        <ng-container *dataGridCellContent />
                    </td>
                </ng-template>
            </tr>
        </ng-template>
    </tbody>
</table>
```

- **`class` and `style` are your element's**, under the structural style, as in React: the
  structural keys win, and the layers and the pinned cells drop the keys the engine writes from
  your static style (never bind those keys yourself). Style the state with the `data-*`
  attributes, or with the part's `state()` (`#own="dataGridCell"`).
- **Listeners compose in the same order**: yours (`(keydown)`, `(click)`, `(pointerdown)`,
  `(copy)`, `(paste)`) run before the grid's on the root, so `preventDefault` in yours cancels a
  key, a sort, a drag or the grid's use of the clipboard.
- **The recursion is templates**: `<ng-template dataGridRows let-row>`,
  `<ng-template dataGridCells let-cell>`; a column's renderers return text or a template with its
  context (`Column<TRow, DataGridNode>`, `templateContent`), which `*dataGridCellContent` renders.
- **Signals all the way**: inputs, outputs (`xChange` for `onXChange`), `state()` and the
  injection functions' `{ state, props }` are signals; nothing changes while the grid scrolls
  within its rendered window.
