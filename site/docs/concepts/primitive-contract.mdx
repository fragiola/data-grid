---
title: The primitive contract
description: The rules every part follows, render instead of asChild, merged refs and handlers, class and style functions, and structural style only.
---

Every part of `DataGrid` (`Root`, `Grid`, `Header`, `HeaderRows`, `HeaderRow`, `HeaderCell`,
`Body`, `Row`, `Cell`, `Empty`) follows the same rules.

## `render`, never `asChild`

`render` replaces the element a part renders. Give it an element, and the part's props are merged
into it; or a function, which receives the props and the part's state.

```tsx title="render.tsx"
<DataGrid.Grid render={<table />} aria-label="People" />

<DataGrid.Cell
    cell={cell}
    render={(props, state) => <td {...props} title={state.active ? "active" : undefined} />}
/>
```

## Props, refs and handlers

- Any prop a part does not use is forwarded to its element (`id`, `aria-*`, `data-*`, handlers).
- `ref` is a plain prop, merged with the part's own.
- A handler you pass runs after the part's own, with three exceptions: `Root` runs the grid's keys
  **after** your `onKeyDown`, its header clicks (sorting) **after** your `onClick`, and a press
  on a column resizer (a drag) **after** your `onPointerDown`, on `Root` or on its `render`
  element; a cell's handlers run before them, so `preventDefault` cancels a key, a sort or a
  drag. See [Keyboard and accessibility](/docs/concepts/keyboard), [Sorting](/docs/concepts/sorting)
  and [Column resizing](/docs/concepts/column-resizing).
- `Body` and `HeaderRow` drop a `transform` from your style: the grid moves them with it. A
  pinned column's cells drop a `transform` and the insets (`top`, `left`, `right`, `bottom`,
  `inset`): they are sticky, the grid writes their `left`. A row's detail drops them too, for the
  same reason.

## `className` and `style`

Both take a value, or a function of the part's state:

```tsx title="row.tsx"
<DataGrid.Row row={row} className={(state) => (state.rowIndex % 2 ? "bg-zinc-50" : undefined)} />
```

## Structural style only

The only inline style a part sets is what places it: `position` (`sticky` on the header, the
empty state, [pinned](/docs/concepts/pinned-columns) cells and a row's
[detail](/docs/guides/master-detail)), `top`, `left`, `width`, `height`, the layers'
`transform`, `display` (`flex` on rows with pinned columns), the root's `overflow`,
`box-sizing`, with [column groups](/docs/concepts/column-groups) a `z-index` between header rows
(an upper row stays above the next), and on a detail `margin-top` (below its row's cells) and,
beside pinned cells, `margin-left` and `flex-shrink`. Your style is
merged under these keys, which always win. Nothing cosmetic is ever set: see the
[`unstyled`](/examples/unstyled) example.

## No text and no names

Parts render their children, or the column's renderer. They set no `aria-label` of their own:
name the grid yourself (`<DataGrid.Grid aria-label="People">`). A
[column's resize handle](/docs/concepts/column-resizing) is an element of yours too:
`useColumnResizer` gives it its props (ARIA, `data-*`, the marker the grid finds it by), never a
name, a place or a look.

## The recursion is yours

`Rows`, `Cells`, `HeaderRows` and `HeaderCells` take a function that renders each item, so every
row and cell is written by you. Without it, they render the default `Row`, `Cell`, `HeaderRow`
and `HeaderCell`.
